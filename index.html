User guide
How to read the chart

The vertical axis is a true price axis — vertical position corresponds directly to price. The orange BTC line is your current price anchor. Levels above are resistances; below are supports.

Confluence zones (shaded bands) appear when multiple independent signals converge within a tight price range. Hover to see each member level's individual price.

When several levels are too close on screen to display individually, they appear as a ×N badge. Hover to see all individual prices.

Green % = level is above current price. Red % = below. Amber = within 0.8%.

Trendlines — how they work

Trendlines are detected algorithmically across three timeframes: Daily (180 candles), Weekly (200 candles), and Monthly (full history).

1. Pivot detection — finds price points that are locally extreme within a window of surrounding candles (pivot highs for resistance, lows for support).

2. Line generation — draws candidate lines between every valid pair of pivots of the same type.

3. Validation — rejects any line breached by price between or after the anchor points.

4. Scoring — ranks valid lines using a weighted heuristic combining: anchor span, recency of second anchor, total history covered, number of touches, and proximity of projected value to current BTC price. This is a heuristic — not a fundamental measure. Adjacent ranks (DTR1 vs DTR2) may be close in quality; the gap between top and bottom is more meaningful.

5. Deduplication — removes near-identical lines, keeps the top 8 per timeframe.

The # rank in each tag reflects score ordering within that timeframe and type. Open the Trendlines section to see the full chart with anchor points and line extensions.

URPD overlay — what it shows

URPD-R (Unrealized Realized Price Distribution, Realized cap weighted) shows how much Bitcoin cost basis is concentrated at each price level.

Each horizontal bar represents a $500 price bin (or $1,000 at All range). Bar width is proportional to the total realized cap of all Bitcoin last moved within that price range — a wider bar means more BTC was acquired near that price, making it a significant support or resistance zone.

The vertical reference line marks where the 100% (strongest) bin would reach. All bars are drawn relative to this fixed scale, so the reference line is always visible even when the strongest bin is outside the current window. Normalization is global across the full Bitcoin distribution.

Toggle ▦ URPD to show or hide the overlay.

On-chain levels

Realized Price (RP) — Average acquisition price of all BTC, weighted by last on-chain movement. Key long-term support in bear markets.

STH Cost Basis (STHCB) — Cost basis of short-term holders (coins moved within ~155 days). Reflects recent speculative positioning.

LTH Cost Basis (LTHCB) — Cost basis of long-term holders (coins unmoved 155+ days). Associated with macro accumulation zones.

True Market Mean (TMM) — Economically weighted cost basis from Cointime Economics. Identifies macro equilibrium zones.

Confluence zones & display groups

Confluence zones use a mean-shift algorithm on the 1D price axis. Each level shifts toward its local density centroid, then levels with converged centers are grouped. Maximum zone width is 2% of price.

Display groups (×N badges) are separate — a readability feature when lines are within ~18px on screen. They carry no analytical meaning. Hover to see individual prices.

Zoom & pan

Ctrl+scroll (or Cmd+scroll on Mac) zooms in/out centered on your cursor's price position.

Click-drag pans the price axis up and down. When zoomed or panned, all price levels are shown regardless of the distance filter.

Double-click resets to the active range preset. The ↺ Reset zoom button also appears when zoomed.
